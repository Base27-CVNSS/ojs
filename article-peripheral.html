Peripheral vs. Center-Seeking Nationalisms and Center-Periphery Relations in Ethiopia

Post-1991 Somali-Oromo Relations

Dawit Biazen Demesse
PhD Candidate, Department of Political Science & International Relations, Addis Ababa University

Abstract

Afar, Gambella, Somali, and Benishangul-Gumuz regions constitute a typology within Ethiopia’s heterogeneous peripheries. Yet previous classifications rarely account for cross-border existence, ethnic demography, and peripheral ethno-nationalism as criteria. This article examines Somali peripheral nationalism in relation to contestation among the post-1991 Ethiopian state center, the TPLF center, and Oromo center-seeking nationalism. Using historical-analytical design, qualitative interviews conducted in Jigjiga and historical-interpretive analysis, the study argues that this triangular dynamic shaped the Somali region’s entry into an asymmetrical reciprocity with the center and reinforced its political peripherality.
