Peripheral vs. Center-Seeking Nationalisms and Center-Periphery Relations in Ethiopia

Post-1991 Somali-Oromo Relations

Dawit Biazen Demesse

https://doi.org/10.4314/ejossah.v21i2.1 ↗

Abstract

Afar, Gambella, Somali, and Benishangul-Gumuz regions constitute a typology within Ethiopia's heterogeneous peripheries. Yet no previous classifications attend to trans-border existence, ethnic demography, and peripheral ethno-nationalism as criteria. These factors expose borderland peripheries to encounters with multiple centers and shape national-level political agency in ways that perpetuate peripherality. Focusing on Somali peripheral nationalism, the paper examines contestation among the post-1991 Ethiopian state center, the TPLF center, and Oromo center-seeking nationalism. Using a historical-analytical design, qualitative interviews in Jigjiga, and historical-interpretive analysis, it finds that scholarship on Somali-Oromo relations has overlooked Somali peripheral nationalism. The resulting triangular dynamic enabled a consequential but asymmetrical political reciprocity while entrenching repressive regional despotism.

Keywords: Center-periphery relations; center-seeking nationalism; peripheral nationalism; Somali-Oromo relations

Technoscientific Rationality

Navigating Dilemmas in the Contemporary Zeitgeist

Nebiyou Ermias Asfaw

https://doi.org/10.4314/ejossah.v21i2.2 ↗

Abstract

The symbiotic integration of science and technology—technoscience—has become a defining feature of the contemporary world. It shapes culture, society, the environment, and how people encounter themselves, others, and the world. This paper examines technoscientific rationality and its dilemmas. It argues that although technoscience plays a significant role in governing the global order, it should not be treated as a totality; its mediating role must be understood within the societies and environments with which it interacts. The paper examines technoscience in contemporary human-world relations, exposes its dual character as pharmakon—both panacea and malady—and proposes ethical and sustainable approaches for the future. Its interaction with contemporary Ethiopians is explored through a multi-method philosophical approach.

Keywords: Ethiopia; mediation; pharmakon; technoscience; technoscientific rationality

Indigenous Values, Beliefs, and Systems for Peacebuilding

The Case of the Jarssaa Gurachaa Inter-Ethnic Indigenous Institution in South-Central Ethiopia

Daniel Mekonnen Yilma

https://doi.org/10.4314/ejossah.v21i2.3 ↗

Abstract

This study explores indigenous values, beliefs, and systems in peacebuilding through Jarssaa Gurachaa, an inter-ethnic institution among the Libido-Mareko, Meskan-Gurage, Oromo, and Silte communities of South-Central Ethiopia. Using focus group discussions, interviews, and observations across woredas in Oromia and Central Ethiopia, it examines a system grounded in values such as Seera and Feroo and rituals including Gudda, Qaala-Maala, and Hodaa. The findings show how these normative frameworks promote cohesion, resolve immediate disputes, and preserve long-term relationships. Their adaptability supports reconciliation without the lingering animosity that formal legal processes may leave unresolved. Jarssaa Gurachaa therefore offers a viable peacebuilding alternative and insights for integrating indigenous knowledge with contemporary frameworks.

Keywords: Conflict-resolution; indigenous peacebuilding; inter-ethnic harmony; Jarssaa Gurachaa; South-Central Ethiopia

Divorce Aftermath through the Lens of Resilience

Lived Experiences of Divorced Custodial, Young Women in Adama, Ethiopia

Girma Megerssa Bedane · Judith Globerman · Debebe Ero

https://doi.org/10.4314/ejossah.v21i2.4 ↗

Abstract

This study investigates the resilience of divorced women in Adama, Ethiopia. Using a phenomenological qualitative approach, it examines the post-divorce experiences of 15 purposively selected custodial single mothers through semi-structured interviews. Participants remained emotionally and socially resilient despite economic hardship. Risk factors included limited income and support networks, conflict with former spouses, lengthy court processes, and stigma. Protective factors included spirituality, self-awareness, commitment to custody, help-seeking, family support, indigenous institutions, and formal services. Four distinctive findings concern sole custody as protection, positive adaptation under economic precarity, the role of traditional social institutions, and changing community attitudes toward divorced women. The results support the development of culturally relevant interventions.

Keywords: Post-divorce lived experiences; resilience perspective; single mothers; sole custody

Archaeological Semiotics of Material Cultures of Gadaa Ritual Performances in Borana and Shewa Zones, Ethiopia

Garedow Abiba · Worku Derara · Alfredo González-Ruibal

https://doi.org/10.4314/ejossah.v21i2.5 ↗

Abstract

This study documents the archaeological semiotics of material cultures used in Gadaa ritual performances in the Borana and Shewa Zones of Oromia. Gadaa is the Oromo traditional governance system recognized by UNESCO in 2016 as intangible heritage. Through qualitative research and purposive sampling, the study identifies iconic, indexical, and symbolic meanings in Gadaa ritual objects. It records both continuity and change in their use and function, with changes associated with modern governance and the spread of plastic and metal objects. Traditional material culture nevertheless remains present across Gadaa ceremonies. A post-processual approach is used to interpret the evidence, and further archaeological investigation is recommended to support documentation and preservation.

Keywords: Archaeological semiotics; ethnoarchaeology; Gadaa ritual performance; material culture
